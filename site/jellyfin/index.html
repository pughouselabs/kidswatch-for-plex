Try the examples below. Nothing connects to your server, and a refresh starts fresh.

01 / What they watch

A library that feels like theirs

Start with Little kids, Kids, or Teens. Then allow a movie they’re ready for or block a show you’d rather skip.

  • Pick “Only titles I allow” for a handpicked library
  • Allow or block a whole series, not individual episodes
  • Unrated titles are excluded by the selection rules
02 / When they watch

School nights and slow Saturdays

Choose daily minutes and watch hours separately. School breaks, Friday movie nights, and Sunday bedtimes can each have their place.

  • One time budget per child across devices
  • Bonus minutes for today, a later bedtime for tonight
  • Pause or resume from the parent dashboard above

Time accounting depends on player reports. Server outages and stale sessions need attention, not a false “all clear.”

03 / Stay in the loop

Know what happened, without hovering

See example watch time, parent changes, and the reason a stop was requested. A request and a confirmed stop should never look the same.

  • Daily activity and a seven-day view
  • Pause, bonus time, and bedtime changes in context
  • Clear warnings when a player cannot be controlled

A known server limit, on every client. Our Jellyfin 12.3 lab still fetched video from blocked titles using known direct video links. Turning playback off also failed to block playback setup and direct streaming. Buffered video may continue, too. Native controls are a best-effort companion, not secure title blocking or a guaranteed cutoff for any client. No client is certified by this preview. Read the player limits.
